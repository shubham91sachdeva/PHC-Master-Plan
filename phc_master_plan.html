Section 1

Vision, Identity & Core Belief

What PHC stands for, what it is trying to change, and why it matters right now.

🎯

Mission

To make health education as foundational as academic education in every Indian school — giving every child the knowledge to understand their body, mind, and emotions from an early age.

🔭

Vision

A generation of Indian children who graduate knowing how to eat, move, rest, think, relate, and manage their wellbeing — for life. India's first truly comprehensive school health literacy programme.

🇮🇳

The Problem

India is the obesity capital of the world. Children are glued to screens. Puberty is undiscussed. Mental health is stigmatised. Financial literacy is absent. Schools teach children everything except how to live healthy.

💡

The Opportunity

CBSE mandates school health programmes but schools lack the curriculum, experts, and structure. PHC delivers the complete package — curriculum, experts, books, training, and events — in one annual partnership.

🏅
Sakshi Malik — Why She Anchors This

Sakshi started her health journey at age 12 as a school student in Rohtak. Her story is not about becoming an Olympian — it is about what happened when a child took her physical and mental health seriously during her school years. That is the message PHC carries to every child: health awareness in school shapes your entire life.

Ages 10–14. Foundational health literacy. One 40-min PHC session per week. One expert visit per month. 10 months, ~40 sessions + events.

🌱
Design Philosophy — Basic Level

This age group is going through puberty, navigating peer pressure, becoming addicted to screens, and forming habits that will last decades. The curriculum is awareness-first — not prescriptive. Every topic is framed as "here is what is happening in your body/mind — here is what it means — here is what you can do about it." No judgment. High curiosity. Story-led where possible.

Section 6

All Topics — Deep Dive Reference

Every topic area expanded — what it covers, why it matters, delivery notes, and sensitive topic handling.

🥗 Nutrition (Proteins, Carbs, Fats, Micronutrients)

Why it's critical: India has among the highest rates of childhood protein deficiency and micronutrient anaemia globally. Most children don't know what a macronutrient is, let alone how much protein they need daily.

Core concepts:

  • Macros: Protein (builds/repairs), Carbohydrates (fuel), Fats (brain, hormones, absorption)
  • Protein needs by age: ~0.9g/kg body weight for 10–14, ~1.1g/kg for 14–18
  • Best protein sources for Indian children: dal, paneer, eggs, soy, sprouts, milk
  • Iron-rich foods: leafy greens, jaggery, sesame — critical for menstruating girls
  • Vitamin D deficiency epidemic in urban Indian children — sunlight + dairy
  • The danger of ultra-processed food — additives, preservatives, empty calories

Delivery tools: Protein Meter app tracker, Food Card game, Canteen Audit exercise, Nutrition Label reading workshop, "Design Your Thali" activity

Expert required: Registered Nutritionist / Dietitian (BSc/MSc Nutrition or RD registered)

💪 Physical Fitness & Exercise Science
  • Aerobic fitness (cardio): running, swimming, cycling, dancing, aerobics — 60 min/day recommended
  • Muscular strength and endurance: bodyweight exercises safe for all ages from Class 5
  • Flexibility: yoga, stretching — especially important during rapid growth phases
  • Balance and coordination: important for injury prevention
  • Sedentary behaviour: sitting for >2 hours = independent health risk factor
  • PE vs PHC: PHC teaches the why and how; PE provides the practice

Key message: Movement is medicine. 30 minutes of daily movement reduces all-cause mortality by 35%. Frame this as science, not punishment.

Expert required: Certified Fitness Trainer (NASM/ACE/ISSA) with experience working with minors

🧠 Mental Health (Stress, Anxiety, Depression Awareness)
  • 1 in 5 Indian adolescents has a diagnosable mental health condition — most go undiagnosed
  • Stress: normal cortisol response vs chronic stress — how to tell the difference
  • Anxiety: generalised, social, performance — symptoms and coping strategies
  • Depression: not "just sadness" — persistent low mood, loss of interest, physical symptoms
  • Stigma in Indian families: most children don't tell parents because of fear of reaction
  • SEL (Social Emotional Learning): embedded in all sessions, not just mental health months

Sensitive handling: Never single out students. Always provide anonymous Q&A option. Counsellor present or on-call during mental health sessions. Clear referral pathway established with school before delivery.

Expert required: RCI-registered Clinical Psychologist / M.Phil Clinical Psychology

🩺 Puberty, Sexual Health & Good Touch Bad Touch
  • Good Touch Bad Touch: Class 5–6 level — body autonomy, safe vs unsafe touch, telling a trusted adult
  • Puberty (Class 6–8): physical changes, emotional changes, hygiene, menstruation for girls
  • Sexual health (Class 9–12): consent, healthy relationships, reproductive anatomy, STI awareness
  • POCSO Act: every student in Class 5–12 should know their legal rights
  • Online safety: digital consent, sexting dangers, grooming awareness

Delivery protocol: Boys and girls in separate sessions for puberty topics. Parent information letter sent before module begins. Opt-out option provided (though not encouraged). All content reviewed by psychologist and paediatrician before delivery.

Experts required: Paediatrician + Child Psychologist. For Class 11–12 sexual health: Gynaecologist for girls, Urologist/General Physician for boys.

💰 Financial Health & Money Literacy
  • Why here? Financial stress is one of the top drivers of adult mental and physical health problems. Starting awareness at 15–17 changes trajectories.
  • Savings accounts: compound interest, SIP basics, why starting early matters
  • UPI and digital payment safety — scam awareness for teenagers
  • Health insurance literacy: premium, deductible, cashless, pre-existing conditions
  • Budgeting: 50/30/20 rule explained simply — needs, wants, savings
  • Debt psychology: why credit cards feel good and are often harmful
  • Career and income: choosing work that pays enough to live healthily

Partnership required: Bank tie-up (HDFC/SBI/ICICI school banking programme). Banks already have school outreach CSR budgets — approach their CSR/financial literacy teams.

🧘 Yoga, Mindfulness & Breathwork
  • Class 1–4 (Foundation): simple stretches, breathing games, body awareness
  • Class 5–8 (Basic): formal asanas (Surya Namaskar, standing poses), pranayama basics, 10-min guided relaxation
  • Class 9–12 (Advanced): advanced pranayama, meditation styles, Yoga Nidra, yoga for specific conditions (anxiety, back pain, insomnia)
  • AYUSH Ministry alignment: mandatory yoga in CBSE schools — PHC provides the certified instructor and structured curriculum
  • Yoga certification: students who complete 2 years of PHC yoga track earn a Yoga Awareness Certificate

Expert required: AYUSH-certified yoga instructor with 200-hr YTT minimum. Ideally RYT-500 for Advanced level delivery. One instructor per school on weekly retainer.

🤝 Social Health, Relationships & Moral Health
  • Peer relationships: friendship quality predicts mental health outcomes more than almost any other factor in adolescence
  • Bullying: 40% of Indian school children report being bullied — recognition, reporting, bystander intervention
  • Moral health: integrity, honesty, accountability — framed as life-skills, not preaching
  • Community health: environmental responsibility, social justice, giving back
  • Digital relationships: online friendships, parasocial relationships, gaming addiction

Delivery format: Circle discussions, role-play, anonymous scenario cards. Not lecture-format. Peer-led for Advanced level.

🦷 Dental Health

Why it's critical: India has among the highest rates of childhood dental caries globally. Studies show 60–80% of Indian school children have cavities. Dental health is almost entirely absent from school curricula despite being directly connected to nutrition, sleep, confidence, and systemic health.

  • Oral anatomy: teeth types (incisors, canines, molars), their functions, adult vs milk teeth
  • Brushing technique: the correct method (2 minutes, circular motion, all surfaces), twice daily, when to replace toothbrush
  • Flossing and tongue cleaning: why brushing alone is insufficient
  • Diet and teeth: how sugar causes caries — acid production by oral bacteria explained simply
  • Foods that protect teeth: calcium-rich foods (milk, paneer, ragi), fibrous vegetables, water
  • Foods that damage teeth: sugary drinks, sticky sweets, excess citrus, acidic sodas
  • Fluoride: why it matters, fluoride toothpaste, fluoride in water
  • Dental checkups: why every child should see a dentist twice a year — most Indian children never do
  • Gum disease: gingivitis basics — signs, prevention, why gums bleed
  • Oral hygiene during puberty: hormonal changes increase gum sensitivity
  • Mouth guards for sports: protection during physical activity
  • Dental emergencies: what to do if a tooth gets knocked out

Partnership required: Dental clinic tie-up for annual dental checkup camps in school. Dental schools (BDS/MDS programmes) often do free school outreach. Target: Manipal College of Dental Sciences, AIIMS Dental, private dental chains (Clove Dental, MyDentist).

Expert required: BDS/MDS registered dentist for annual camp. Dental hygienist for monthly awareness sessions.

Activity: "Plaque Disclosure Tablet" experiment — students use disclosing tablets to see exactly where they missed while brushing. Highly effective, memorable, and fun.

🧼 Personal Hygiene

Why it's critical: Personal hygiene is the single most impactful public health behaviour. Proper hand hygiene alone prevents 50% of infectious disease transmission. Yet many Indian children are never taught the correct methods or the science behind why hygiene matters.

Sub-topics covered:
  • Hand hygiene: The 6-step WHO handwashing technique, when to wash hands (before eating, after toilet, after touching animals, after school), soap vs sanitiser, the difference between washing and rinsing
  • Bathing and skin care: Daily bathing importance, proper technique, skin pH and why harsh soaps harm it, moisturising in dry seasons, acne hygiene (gentle cleansing vs scrubbing)
  • Hair care: Frequency of washing, scalp hygiene, lice prevention and treatment (destigmatised), oiling practices — when they help vs create problems
  • Nail care: Trimming nails, cleaning under nails, why long nails harbour bacteria, nail-biting and its health implications
  • Foot hygiene: Washing between toes, drying properly, fungal infections (athlete's foot), changing socks daily, proper footwear
  • Clothing hygiene: Changing clothes daily, washing uniforms, importance of clean undergarments, fabric breathability and skin health
  • Toilet hygiene: Proper wiping technique, handwashing after toilet, toilet seat hygiene (especially in school toilets), avoiding holding urine too long
  • Ear and eye hygiene: Safe ear cleaning (no cotton buds inside the canal), eye washing, screen use and eye strain
  • Respiratory hygiene: Coughing/sneezing etiquette, masking when unwell, nose blowing correctly
  • Sweat and body odour: Puberty and increased sweating — deodorant vs antiperspirant, when body odour is normal vs a sign of illness, fabric choices

Expert required: Paediatrician for school hygiene sessions. School nurse / health worker for ongoing reinforcement.

Delivery approach: Highly visual and activity-based. Handwashing demonstrations with UV powder. Hygiene pledge boards in school toilets. Monthly hygiene spot-checks in collaboration with school health prefects.

🩸 Menstrual Health & Pee Safe Sanitary Kits

Why it's critical: 1 in 4 Indian girls drops out of school after her first period. 88% of Indian girls use unsafe methods (cloth, ash, sand) during menstruation due to lack of access to sanitary products and awareness. This is a public health emergency — and PHC directly addresses it.

Curriculum content:
  • Menstruation basics: what it is, why it happens, the hormonal cycle explained in simple terms
  • Cycle awareness: typical cycle length (21–35 days is normal), tracking your period, what variations are normal
  • Sanitary products: types — sanitary napkins, tampons, menstrual cups, period underwear — how each works, pros and cons
  • Proper disposal of sanitary products: hygiene, environment, the incinerator in school toilets
  • Menstrual hygiene: changing pads every 4–6 hours, washing hands before and after, bathing during periods (dispelling myths)
  • Period pain (dysmenorrhea): what is normal, heat pads, hydration, gentle movement vs bed rest — when pain means you should see a doctor
  • Period myths debunked: not "impure", not shameful, no restrictions on bathing/exercise/food during periods
  • Premenstrual symptoms (PMS): mood changes, bloating, cramps — understanding your body, not fighting it
  • Period and school: how to manage periods in school, asking for help, using the sick room
  • For boys: why understanding menstruation makes you a better friend, brother, and ally
  • Irregular periods: when to tell a parent or doctor
Pee Safe Partnership — School Sanitary Kit Programme:
  • Pee Safe is India's leading feminine hygiene brand with an active school outreach programme
  • PHC will partner with Pee Safe to provide every girl student (Class 6 onwards) with a Sanitary Kit containing: sanitary napkins (sample pack), a hygiene pouch, menstrual health pamphlet, and a Pee Safe toilet seat sanitizer spray for school toilet use
  • Kits distributed at the start of the academic year alongside the PHC workbook
  • Pee Safe to co-brand hygiene content in the PHC Student Workbook (girls' edition insert)
  • Pee Safe vending machine installation in girls' toilets (schools with 200+ girl students)
  • Pee Safe representatives conduct one annual "Menstrual Health Awareness" session for Class 6–12 girls
  • Approach: Pee Safe CSR team + B2B school partnership division. They have precedent for school programmes across 500+ schools.

Expert required: Gynaecologist for annual menstrual health session. School nurse for ongoing support. Pee Safe representative for product education.

Sensitive handling: All menstrual health content delivered in gender-separated sessions. Male teachers should not lead these sessions. Female PHC coordinator or female expert required for delivery.

Section 9

Teacher Training Programme

Teachers are the daily delivery engine. PHC only works if teachers are confident, informed, and equipped to handle every topic including sensitive ones.

⚠️
This is the most critical implementation step

A curriculum without trained teachers is a printed book in a cupboard. Every PHC school must have at least one PHC-trained teacher before the programme begins. No exceptions.

👩‍🏫
Delivery Split: ~30 Teacher-Led + ~10 Expert-Led Sessions a Year

The model is built so one trained class teacher can run the great majority of the 40 weekly sessions solo, using a scripted "Teacher Cue" line on every session card. A visiting subject expert (dentist, fitness trainer, psychologist, gynaecologist, yoga instructor, bank rep) is sent in roughly once a month for the ~10 sessions a year that genuinely need specialist judgement — puberty, safety, menstrual health, sports form, mental health. This keeps the per-school cost of the expert roster bounded while every session still stays hands-on. Full week-by-week facilitator assignment: see phc_40_week_curriculum.html.

❓ What does the teacher training cover?

Module 1: PHC Curriculum Overview (3 hours)

  • The philosophy behind PHC — awareness, not performance
  • How to use the Teacher Guide and Student Workbook
  • Annual calendar overview — what happens each month

Module 2: Health Literacy for Teachers (4 hours)

  • Nutrition basics — so teachers can answer student questions confidently
  • Mental health awareness — recognising signs of distress in students
  • Puberty and body changes — how to handle questions without discomfort

Module 3: Sensitive Topic Handling (2 hours)

  • Good Touch Bad Touch — delivery protocol for Class 5–6
  • Mental health crisis: what to do if a student discloses self-harm or suicidal thoughts
  • When to involve the school counsellor or principal
  • Parent communication — templates and tone

Module 4: Practical Delivery (2 hours)

  • Live practice: each teacher delivers one session with feedback
  • Q&A handling practice — role-play difficult student questions
  • Using the PHC app — entering data, tracking student engagement

Institutional Partnerships & Tie-ups

Every external institution needed — hospitals, banks, yoga bodies, government, media — and how to approach them.

🏥 Hospital / Healthcare Networks

Why needed: Source of paediatricians, psychologists, gynaecologists, and physiotherapists for expert visits. Health checkup camps require a medical team.

Target Hospitals:
  • Apollo Hospitals — has a community health / school health CSR division
  • Fortis Healthcare — school health tie-ups precedent exists
  • Max Healthcare — Delhi/NCR strong
  • Manipal Hospitals — South India scale
  • Government District Hospitals — for government school partnerships

Approach: CSR department first. PHC offers brand association + guaranteed school access (hard for hospitals to get independently). Structure: hospital provides experts at subsidised rate; PHC provides school access and brand visibility. Sign MOU with 1-year term.

Ask: Minimum 1 paediatrician + 1 psychologist per city. 2 health camp days per school per year. Hospital branding on Health Report Card.

🏦 Banks (Financial Literacy Tie-up)

Why needed: Financial health curriculum for Class 9–12 requires a bank expert. Banks also benefit — school banking programme onboards future customers.

Target Banks:
  • HDFC Bank — has "Parivartan" CSR programme with strong school focus
  • SBI — School Savings Bank account programme already exists
  • ICICI Bank — "Financial Literacy" is a stated CSR pillar
  • Kotak Mahindra Bank — youth banking initiatives

Approach: CSR team + Retail Banking division simultaneously. PHC offers: captive audience of 500+ students per school, parent engagement touchpoint, brand visibility in workbook and app. Bank provides: financial literacy specialist for sessions, school banking account setup, co-branded financial health content in workbook.

🧘 Yoga Bodies & Wellness Networks
  • Ministry of AYUSH — official alignment gives PHC government credibility. Apply for AYUSH recognition of PHC yoga curriculum.
